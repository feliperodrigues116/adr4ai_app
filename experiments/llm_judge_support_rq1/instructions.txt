Architectural decision principles:
- Architectural pattern selection may admit multiple valid and defensible solutions. A pattern need not be the best, unique, or most obvious solution to be applicable. Do not penalize a decision merely because alternatives or other architectural interpretations are possible.

- A possible implementation, test, improvement, or supporting use of a pattern is not by itself evidence that the requirement exhibits the architectural need it addresses. Absence of contradictory evidence is not positive applicability evidence.
- Do not assume GPT's rationale is correct or incorrect. Examine its relationship to the available evidence. Fluent wording is not automatically sufficient evidence, and a rationale must not be discounted merely because GPT produced it.

Category-specific criteria:

Do not penalize appropriate acknowledgment of uncertainty. Where sufficient positive evidence or evidence of incompatibility exists, assess whether the insufficient-information decision remains defensible.
